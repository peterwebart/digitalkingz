How Much Does a Business Website Cost? Complete
Pricing Guide
Introduction: How Much Should You Budget for a Business Website?
The cost of a business website can range from a few hundred euros for a simple DIY setup to
hundreds of thousands for a multilingual, integrated enterprise platform. For planning
purposes, many small businesses spend roughly €3,000–€15,000 on a professional website,
while a strategic B2B website often requires €15,000–€75,000 and a complex custom platform
can exceed €100,000.
Those figures are planning ranges, not universal prices. The final website development cost
depends on the scope, number of templates and pages, content, design, integrations,
technology, security, accessibility, performance requirements, location of the delivery team,
and ongoing support model. A five-page brochure site and a website connected to a CRM,
product database, customer portal, analytics platform, and multilingual content system are
both “business websites,” but they are different projects.
This guide explains how much a business website costs by separating one-time development
expenses from recurring operating costs. It also shows how to compare website development
quotes, identify hidden costs, plan a realistic budget, and evaluate whether a website
investment can produce measurable commercial value.
Important: The most useful website budget is not the cheapest number. It is a
documented estimate tied to requirements, assumptions, deliverables, responsibilities,
risks, and post-launch ownership.

What Factors Determine Business Website Cost?
Website pricing is mainly the cost of decisions, specialist time, technical risk, and ongoing
responsibility. A website becomes more expensive when it must support more audiences,
content, functionality, data, integrations, markets, or risk controls.

How does website scope affect the price?
Scope includes more than the number of pages. It includes page types, features, user roles,
workflows, integrations, content volume, markets, and quality requirements.

Each addition creates design, development, content, testing, governance, and maintenance
work. “Can you add one more feature?” is often not a small question if it changes the data
model, permissions, integrations, or user experience.

How do strategy and discovery affect website development cost?
Discovery may include stakeholder interviews, customer research, competitor analysis,
content audits, analytics review, technical audits, requirements workshops, sitemap
development, user-journey mapping, and project planning.
Some providers remove discovery from a quote to appear cheaper. The work does not
disappear; it often reappears as rework, unclear decisions, or change requests during
development.
Discovery is particularly valuable when:
Several departments own different website requirements.
The company sells to technical or regulated buyers.
The website is replacing an older platform.
A URL migration could affect search visibility.
The site needs CRM, ERP, product, or customer-portal integrations.
Different countries or languages are involved.
The project has multiple decision makers.
A short discovery phase may cost less than a full strategy engagement, but it should still
produce useful outputs: goals, audience priorities, requirements, sitemap, risks, assumptions,
and a proposed delivery plan.

Content is one of the most underestimated parts of website development pricing. Someone

material, migrate content, and coordinate translations, the budget must reflect that work.

How Much Does Each Type of Business Website Cost?
The following ranges are useful for early planning. They are not fixed tariffs. A website quote
should become more specific after the provider understands the requirements, content
responsibility, integrations, and acceptance criteria.

How much does a DIY or website-builder website cost?
A DIY website may cost approximately €100–€2,500 in the first year, depending on the platform,
domain, template, premium features, email, plugins, stock media, and ecommerce
requirements. Ongoing costs may range from a modest monthly subscription to a much higher
amount for advanced commerce, marketing, or collaboration features.
A DIY approach may be suitable when:
The business needs a simple presence quickly.
The owner can manage content and basic design.
The number of pages is small.
Integrations are limited.
The business can accept platform constraints.
There is no complex customer portal or workflow.
Typical hidden costs include owner time, premium templates, plugins, stock images,
copywriting, technical troubleshooting, SEO configuration, accessibility fixes, and future
migration.

How much does a freelancer-built website cost?
A freelancer-built business website may cost roughly €2,000–€15,000, with simple projects at
the lower end and custom design, stronger content, integrations, or technical requirements at
the higher end.
Freelancers can offer:
Direct communication

Lower overhead
Flexible scope
Strong personal accountability
Good value for focused projects
Risks include limited availability, dependence on one person, fewer specialist capabilities,
weaker continuity, and uncertain support during illness or other commitments. Ask how the
freelancer handles backups, security updates, handover, source files, analytics access, and
post-launch support.

How much does a professional small-business website cost?
A professional small-business website commonly falls around €5,000–€25,000 when it includes
strategy, custom or adapted design, CMS setup, copy support, responsive development, basic
SEO, analytics, forms, and launch QA.
A lower-cost professional project may use:
A proven CMS
A limited number of page templates
Client-provided content
Standard integrations
A small design system
Limited custom functionality
A higher-cost project may include customer research, custom positioning, original
photography, technical copywriting, multilingual pages, advanced forms, CRM integration,
industry pages, case studies, performance optimization, and accessibility testing.

How much does a B2B website cost?
A serious B2B website often costs approximately €15,000–€75,000. More complex B2B platforms
can exceed €100,000.
B2B websites cost more when they must support:
Multiple buying-committee roles

Case-study and proof libraries

CRM and marketing automation
Lead routing and qualification
Account-based marketing

Technical documentation

A B2B website should not be priced only by the number of pages. A technically important
architecture guide or integration page may require more expertise than several basic company
pages.

How much does a custom website cost?
A custom website or web platform may cost approximately €30,000–€150,000 or more. The price
rises when the website includes custom workflows, user accounts, portals, product
configuration, advanced search, data visualization, personalization, multiple APIs, or unusual
business rules.
Custom development may be justified when:
A standard CMS cannot support the required workflow.
The website is part of the product or customer experience.
The business needs a portal or authenticated application.
Multiple systems must be synchronized.
The user journey is a competitive differentiator.
Performance, security, or compliance requirements are unusually strict.
Custom does not mean every element must be built from scratch. Reusing stable frameworks,
components, APIs, and managed services can reduce risk while preserving necessary
differentiation.

A basic ecommerce website may cost approximately €10,000–€30,000. A more complex

An enterprise website platform may cost approximately €75,000–€500,000 or more over the

How Do Website Providers Charge?
The commercial model affects risk, flexibility, and how easily you can compare proposals.

How does hourly website pricing work?
Hourly pricing charges for time by role or blended team rate. It can work well when the scope
is uncertain, the project is iterative, or ongoing optimization is expected.
Advantages:
Flexible scope
Easier to adapt to new information
Suitable for maintenance and experimentation
Transparent connection between work and time
Risks:
Final cost may be uncertain
Poorly managed projects can consume hours without enough progress
Comparing teams requires understanding roles and rates
The client may carry more scope-management responsibility
Request an estimated range, assumptions, role mix, reporting method, and approval rules for
exceeding the estimate.

What is fixed-price website development?
A fixed-price project sets a price for an agreed scope and deliverables. It can help with budget
approval and procurement comparison.
A fixed price is reliable only when the scope is defined. If requirements are unclear, vendors
may protect their margin through exclusions, change requests, or reduced depth.
Define:
Deliverables
Page and template count
Functional requirements
Integrations
Content responsibilities
Revision rounds
Testing scope
Acceptance criteria
Timeline dependencies
Change-request process
Warranty period

What is a monthly retainer?
A retainer provides a recurring amount of capacity or service. It may cover maintenance,
content, SEO, analytics, optimization, support, or ongoing development.
A retainer is useful after launch when the website needs continuous attention. It should specify
hours or outputs, roles, response times, rollover rules, reporting, and priority handling.

What is a value-based or milestone-based model?
Milestone-based pricing links payments to agreed outputs or project phases, such as strategy
approval, design approval, development completion, QA, and launch. Value-based pricing may
connect fees to the value or commercial importance of the work rather than hours alone.
For procurement, the most important issue is clarity: what is being delivered, when, by whom,
with what acceptance conditions, and under what assumptions?

How do you separate launch scope from future improvements?
Divide requirements into three groups:
Launch-critical: Necessary for the initial business objective, compliance, user task, or
technical operation.
Post-launch priority: Valuable improvements that can be delivered after the core
experience is stable.
Exploratory: Ideas that require research, validation, or a future business case.
This prevents the project from either launching with too little value or becoming so large that
delivery stalls.

What contingency should a website project include?
A contingency of roughly 10%–20% may be reasonable for a well-defined project, with more
required when requirements are uncertain, integrations are complex, migration is large, or
stakeholders are numerous. The correct amount depends on risk.
Use contingency for known uncertainty—not for avoidable poor planning. A risk register
should explain what may change the budget:
Unclear requirements
Content not ready
Integration documentation missing
Legacy data quality
Translation volume
Security findings
Approval delays

How do content and stakeholder delays affect the budget?
Delays can create cost through idle capacity, rescheduling, extended project management,
expiring licenses, missed launch windows, and compressed testing. Assign content owners and
approval deadlines before development depends on them.
A website project should show dependencies such as:

API access before integration testing

How Can You Reduce Website Development Costs Without Reducing Quality?
Cost control is about reducing unnecessary complexity and improving decisions, not removing
responsible engineering.

When should you use an existing CMS or template?
Use an established CMS or template when it meets the business requirements and the company
can operate it reliably. Templates can reduce design and development effort, especially for
simple sites.
Customize only where differentiation or user needs justify it. Excessive customization
increases maintenance, testing, upgrade, and vendor-dependency costs.

Internal teams can reduce external cost by providing:

Subject-matter experts

Do not assign technical, copywriting, accessibility, SEO, or security work internally unless the
team has the relevant capability and capacity. “We can do it ourselves” is economical only
when the work is completed to the necessary standard.

Audit and remove unnecessary pages before migration.

Why is cutting accessibility, security, or performance usually a false economy?
These areas protect users, reputation, operations, and future cost. Deferring them can create
remediation, legal, incident-response, lost conversion, or replatforming expenses. Build
reasonable requirements into the original scope and test them before launch.

What Website Investment Produces the Best Business Value?
The most valuable website investment improves an important user decision or business
process. It may not be the most visually elaborate site.

How do you measure website-influenced pipeline?
Define what counts as meaningful website engagement. Options may include viewing highintent technical pages, returning to product content, downloading an implementation guide,
attending a webinar, submitting an assessment, or engaging with a target-account journey.
Connect the website to CRM campaign records where appropriate, use consistent UTMs,
document attribution windows, and distinguish sourced from influenced opportunities. Do not
claim that every visitor influenced revenue.

What Common Website Budgeting Mistakes Should You Avoid?
Choosing the cheapest quote without comparing scope
A low quote may exclude strategy, content, migration, integrations, accessibility, security, or
support. Compare normalized scope and first-year cost.

Ignoring content
An attractive website with weak, missing, or unapproved content will not support buyers.
Budget for writing, technical review, customer evidence, translation, media, and migration.

Treating strategy as optional
Without goals, audience priorities, and requirements, the project may deliver a polished site
that does not support the business.

Underestimating integrations
A connection to a CRM, ERP, payment provider, product database, or portal creates
development, testing, monitoring, and maintenance work.

Forgetting recurring costs
Hosting, licenses, security, backups, support, content, SEO, and optimization can exceed the
initial build cost over time.

Leaving SEO until after launch
Late SEO work can require URL changes, content rewriting, redirects, template changes, and
recovery from lost visibility.

Cutting security and accessibility
These are quality and risk requirements, not decorative extras. Include them in scope and
acceptance criteria.

Failing to define ownership
Clarify who owns the domain, accounts, code, design files, content, data, backups, analytics,
and maintenance access.

Allowing unlimited revisions
Unlimited revisions create unclear scope and make quality difficult to manage. Define review
rounds, decision owners, and change control.

Not including contingency
Unexpected dependencies are common. Reserve an appropriate contingency based on
uncertainty and risk.

Assuming a website is finished at launch
Launch creates a baseline. The website still needs monitoring, content updates, security
maintenance, SEO work, and optimization.

Measuring traffic instead of commercial outcomes
Traffic is useful context but not business value by itself. Measure audience quality, meaningful
actions, pipeline influence, revenue, customer outcomes, and technical health.

What 90-Day Website Budget and Planning Process Should You Follow?
What should happen in the first 30 days?
1. Define the business objective and success metrics.
2. Identify audiences, buying committees, and user tasks.
3. Audit the current website, content, technology, analytics, SEO, accessibility, and security.
4. List required pages, features, integrations, and content.
5. Separate launch-critical scope from future improvements.
6. Establish an initial budget range and contingency.
7. Create a requirements document and request comparable proposals.

What should happen from days 31 to 60?
1. Evaluate providers against normalized requirements.
2. Confirm the CMS, technology, hosting, and integration approach.
3. Finalize the sitemap, content plan, responsibilities, and timeline.
4. Approve design direction and key templates.
5. Confirm data ownership, access, security, accessibility, SEO, and performance
requirements.
6. Define change control, acceptance criteria, and payment milestones.
7. Confirm year-one operating costs.

What should happen from days 61 to 90?
1. Begin content, design, and development.
2. Track progress against scope, budget, and risk.
3. Review milestones using real content and representative user journeys.
4. Test priority integrations and conversion paths.
5. Prepare migration, SEO, accessibility, security, and launch plans.
6. Confirm maintenance, monitoring, training, and ownership.
7. Update the budget as assumptions become facts.

FAQ
How much does it cost to build a business website?
A simple professional business website may cost approximately €3,000–€15,000. A more
complete small-business website may cost €5,000–€25,000, a B2B website €15,000–€75,000, and a
custom or enterprise platform €75,000–€500,000 or more. The actual price depends on scope,
content, integrations, technology, location, and support.

What is the average cost of a professional website?
There is no reliable universal average because “professional website” covers very different
projects. For planning, many small businesses should consider a low-to-mid five-figure budget
if they need strategy, custom design, content support, SEO, integrations, and proper QA rather
than a basic template setup.

How much does a small business website cost?
A small business website may cost approximately €2,000–€15,000 when built by a freelancer or
using a standard platform. A more strategic project with custom design, professional content,
SEO, integrations, and conversion optimization may cost €15,000–€25,000 or more.

How much does a B2B website cost?
A B2B website commonly costs approximately €15,000–€75,000. Costs increase when the
website must support technical evaluation, multiple industries, case studies, security content,
CRM integration, marketing automation, multilingual markets, advanced governance, or
complex lead qualification.

How much does a custom website cost?
A custom website may cost approximately €30,000–€150,000 or more. Custom portals, product
configurators, advanced search, multiple APIs, user accounts, personalization, and complex
workflows can increase the cost substantially.

How much does an ecommerce website cost?
A basic ecommerce site may cost approximately €10,000–€30,000. More complex ecommerce
websites with large catalogs, product configuration, subscriptions, inventory or ERP
integration, international tax, advanced search, and customer accounts may cost €30,000–
€150,000 or more.

How much does a website cost per month?
Basic hosting, domain, updates, and backups may cost roughly €50–€300 per month.
Professional B2B maintenance and support may cost €300–€2,000 or more per month. Complex
ecommerce and enterprise platforms may require several thousand euros per month or a
dedicated internal team.

What is included in website development pricing?
A quote may include strategy, design, development, CMS setup, content entry, integrations,
SEO, analytics, testing, launch, training, and support. However, providers define scope
differently. Ask for a detailed list of inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, recurring costs, and
change-request rules.

Why do website costs vary so much?
Website projects vary in audience, content volume, page types, functionality, integrations,
markets, security, accessibility, performance, agency location, stakeholder complexity, and
ongoing support. A five-page brochure site and an integrated enterprise platform are not
comparable projects.

Is it cheaper to build a website yourself?
DIY platforms reduce direct cash cost, but they require owner time and may create limitations
in design, performance, integrations, SEO, accessibility, and scalability. DIY can be sensible for
a simple site; it is less suitable when the website supports complex B2B evaluation or critical
business workflows.

Should I hire a freelancer or a website agency?
A freelancer may offer good value and direct communication for a focused project. An agency
may provide broader strategy, UX, content, development, SEO, security, analytics, and projectmanagement capabilities. Choose based on required expertise, risk, scope, continuity, and
post-launch needs—not price alone.

How much does website maintenance cost?
Basic maintenance may cost €50–€300 per month. Professional maintenance with security,
analytics, content, SEO, support, and development may cost €300–€2,000 or more per month.
Ecommerce and enterprise websites can require significantly more.

How much does website hosting cost?
Hosting can range from a low monthly subscription for a simple site to hundreds or thousands
per month for high-traffic, high-availability, enterprise, or regulated systems. Evaluate
performance, backups, security, support, scaling, environment management, and data location
—not only the monthly price.

How long does it take to build a business website?
A simple website may take days or weeks. A professional small-business site may take four to
twelve weeks. A B2B website may take two to six months, while a custom or enterprise platform
can take six to eighteen months or longer. Content readiness and decision speed strongly affect
the timeline.

What should a website development quote include?
It should include objectives, scope, page and template assumptions, functionality, integrations,
content responsibilities, SEO, accessibility, security, performance, QA, timeline, dependencies,
team, pricing, payment milestones, warranty, maintenance, ownership, handover, exclusions,
and recurring costs.

How do I compare website development proposals?
Normalize each proposal against the same requirements. Compare templates, page population,
content writing, migration, integrations, SEO, accessibility, security, QA, training, warranty,
recurring technology, maintenance, ownership, and change-request pricing. Evaluate the total
first-year cost and risk.

What makes a website more expensive?
Custom functionality, integrations, multilingual support, large content migration, ecommerce,
customer portals, advanced search, personalization, security, accessibility, performance,
multiple stakeholder groups, international markets, and complex governance are common cost
drivers.

How can I reduce website development costs?
Define requirements early, prioritize launch scope, use a suitable existing CMS, prepare
content, reduce unnecessary page variations, reuse stable components, remove obsolete
content, provide timely approvals, and avoid speculative features. Do not reduce costs by
removing essential security, accessibility, performance, or SEO work.

Is a website a business expense?
Website development, hosting, software, maintenance, content, marketing, and professional
services may be treated as business expenses depending on jurisdiction, accounting policy, and
how the costs are classified. Consult a qualified accountant for your specific situation.

How do I calculate website ROI?
Define the website objective, baseline, conversion and quality measures, attribution approach,
costs, and review period. Compare incremental gross profit or validated operational value with
total website cost. Be transparent about the website’s role alongside sales, marketing, product,
and market conditions.
